## Patterns: what has won with Monad judges

1. **Consumer-legible products beat infrastructure.** The evm/accathon top 3 were a social-signal trading app, event crowdfunding/ticketing and Apple-Pay-style payments. None of them was a protocol primitive.
2. **"Crypto that doesn't feel like crypto."** Examples: OwnPay, Troia (pay in lira), TypeX (payments in the keyboard), Sparkball (plays down Web3), Kizzy (mobile PWA). This matches the Track 2 prompt directly.
3. **Speculation plus social/culture is Monad's native taste.** Examples: KiSignals, Kizzy, RareBetSports, CRSH, NoJohns wagering, the Moltiverse token track, and livestream/cultural prediction markets. Markets on non-financial outcomes keep winning.
4. **Agents win when they are playful, visible and transacting.** Examples: games, arenas, dating and life sims (Moltiverse), or a transparent trading agent (OpenAlice). "Infra for agents" alone rarely topped the lists. Ship something a judge can watch.
5. **Real-time UX that shows off speed.** Examples: in-play markets, 4-minute matches, tap-the-chart trading (Blink.cash), per-second and micro-streaming payments (TickPay).
6. **Sponsor stacking is normal.** Gorillionaire placed 4th overall and also won the Envio bounty, and KiSignals won 1st plus the 0x bounty. Integrating 2–3 sponsor tools (Envio, Privy/Dynamic, Kuru/Perpl, AUSD) is table stakes.
7. **Teams that later become companies get pulled forward.** Examples: Kizzy → App Hub, Blocklive/StageFun, KiFi, and the snubeaver hire. Metropolis explicitly frames itself as "startups" with a residency afterwards. Judges are mostly VCs (see judges-theses.md), so they score on "could this be a company?"
