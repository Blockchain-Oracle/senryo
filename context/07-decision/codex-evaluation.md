# Independent product evaluation: Metropolis A, B, and C

**Research date:** 29 September 2026. **Recommendation:** **C, entered in Track 01**: a mobile trading product for real-world markets with one *risk-accounted* collateral vault and a card as a way to spend **free** borrowing power. The market and its session-aware pricing are the core product. The card is a useful extension, not the judging claim. This is a product recommendation, not a claim that a card issuer, oracle licence, market maker, or live user has been secured.

## Short answers to Q1–Q5

1. **A — card authorization.** A normal swipe reaches the merchant acquirer, network, issuer/processor, and then (only in a partner-managed or external-authorization program) our decision endpoint. Reap's published external mode gives that endpoint **1.6 seconds** to return `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`, including all network and computation time; timeout fails closed. An onchain borrow finalized before that reply is a plausible experiment on Monad, **not an established Rain capability or guaranteed latency**. Rain publicly offers partner-managed authorization *and*, separately, real-time wallet funding in a Rain-managed beta; the latter's published launch assets are USDC on Base/Arbitrum and USDT0 on Plasma. Rain supports Monad card programs, but public evidence does not establish that this beta or our proposed borrow flow is enabled on Monad. [Reap request](https://docs.reap.global/transactions/external-authorization/authorization-request.md), [Reap overview](https://docs.reap.global/transactions/external-authorization/overview.md), [Rain program models](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/launch-a-card-program-with-rain), [Rain real-time funding](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/introducing-real-time-funding), [Rain–Monad announcement](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/rain-integrates-monad-bringing-card-programs-to-the-financial-layer-of-the-internet).
2. **B — listing layer.** It requires a listing/bond registry, execution venue, session-aware oracle/mark adapter, margin and funding accounting, liquidations, insurance/backstop, and market makers. HIP-3 is a **deployer-operated venue on HyperCore**, not just a factory: deployers set oracles and operate markets while the shared exchange supplies order books and margining. A new Monad contract cannot make Perpl permissionlessly list a market; that needs a Perpl agreement, or our own execution engine. Per-block *settlement* of funding adds little: continuous accrual with periodic settlement already captures the economics. The genuine Monad angle is fast, auditable fills and contract-enforced session, freshness, gap and corporate-action controls. [HIP-3 specification](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals), [Pyth price availability](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/best-practices), [GMX liquidity design](https://docs.gmx.io/docs/providing-liquidity/).
3. **C — one app.** Yes, card plus trading is a proven *app category*: Bybit documents both collateralized card spending and a unified trading account. It does **not** prove one identical balance is safely pledged twice; Bybit's card spending uses its Funding Account, while the trading account has separate margin accounting. Our useful invention is one vault with an explicit, atomic spendable-equity calculation. A card hold or new debt reduces trading headroom immediately; an open position reduces card headroom immediately. [Bybit Card Pay Later](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/FAQ-Pay-Later-on-Bybit-Card), [Bybit trading account](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/FAQ-Unified-Trading-Account), [Bybit card transactions](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/How-Bybit-Card-Transactions-Work).
4. **Choice and track.** Choose **C/Track 01**, with the concise promise: **“Trade real-world markets on Monad; spend only the collateral your positions have not committed.”** Track 01 expressly accepts market structures and mobile trading experiences. Track 02 says its core value cannot be trading. The portal permits one project per participant and one track per project. The two published judging rubrics differ; the tables below score both as conditional design potential, not a prediction of a judge's score. [Captured Track 01](/Users/abu/dev/hackathon/metropolis/context/_portal/tracks/onchain-finance.md), [captured Track 02](/Users/abu/dev/hackathon/metropolis/context/_portal/tracks/consumer-payments.md), [captured rules](/Users/abu/dev/hackathon/metropolis/context/_portal/pages/rules.md).
5. **Recommended stack and build order.** Expo/React Native development builds on iOS and Android; Mera passkeys with platform WebAuthn and `viem`; a dedicated authorization/ledger service; Monad contracts for vault, unified risk, RWA markets, card holds/debt, liquidation and insurance; Pyth/Chainlink data with session verification; an event indexer. Build the **risk invariant and a complete trade** first, then card authorization and reconciliation, then native wallet provisioning. Mera's published React Native support is iOS 18+ and Android 9+; Mera derives an EVM account from WebAuthn PRF and is not itself an onchain P256-verifying smart account. [Mera repository](https://github.com/category-labs/mera), [Monad Mera guide](https://docs.monad.xyz/guides/mera/react-native), [Monad differences](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/differences.md).

## What the evidence changes

### A. The card is a credit and reconciliation system, not a speed slogan

**Exact path for the proposed partner-managed program:** card tap → merchant acquirer → Visa/network → Rain issuer processor → Rain's local card, program, fraud and reserve checks → signed authorization request to our endpoint → our signature verification, idempotency lookup, fresh risk state, and **atomic reservation** → possible onchain borrow/escrow transaction → `APPROVE` or `DECLINE` response → issuer's final decision → network/acquirer/terminal. An approved authorization is a promise to pay, **not merchant settlement**. Clearing may differ from the authorized amount; reversals, incremental authorizations, refunds and chargebacks require asynchronous ledger reconciliation. Reap publishes this exact division of responsibility for its own external mode, including the master collateral check *after* our approval. Rain publicly confirms partner-managed webhooks and our responsibility for reserves and ledger, but its detailed API is access-restricted after commercial scoping and NDA. Therefore the Reap wire contract must **not** be passed off as Rain's exact schema or SLA. [Reap overview](https://docs.reap.global/transactions/external-authorization/overview.md), [request](https://docs.reap.global/transactions/external-authorization/authorization-request.md), [handling and holds](https://docs.reap.global/transactions/external-authorization/handling-requests.md), [Rain program guide](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/launch-a-card-program-with-rain), [Rain authorization/clearing explanation](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/the-anatomy-of-a-stablecoin-card-swipe).

**Does finality fit?** Reap's **1.6-second** window applies to *our entire response*, not to chain inclusion alone. Rain's public material calls card authorizations a roughly **two-second** interaction, but publishes no partner webhook deadline. Monad's currently published overview says **400 ms blocks / 800 ms finality**; the captured portal repeats those figures, while the local chain observation in the project notes on **29 September 2026** was approximately **300 ms blocks / 600 ms finality**. Neither figure includes issuer-to-webhook transit, signature verification, transaction simulation, RPC submission, fee selection, state contention, finalized-receipt observation or response transit. `latest` is speculative; require `finalized` for a pre-approval proof. The claim succeeds only if measured p99 end-to-end time plus issuer margin fits the *actual contracted* deadline and authorization/chain failures are reconciled without double borrowing. [Monad published overview](https://docs.monad.xyz/), [Monad transaction states](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/transactions.md), [captured Track 01](/Users/abu/dev/hackathon/metropolis/context/_portal/tracks/onchain-finance.md), [Reap deadline](https://docs.reap.global/transactions/external-authorization/authorization-request.md). The **300/600** observation is a dated local measurement, not a guaranteed chain SLA: [project chain notes](/Users/abu/dev/hackathon/metropolis/context/02-monad/differences-from-ethereum.md).

**A safe route if that measurement fails:** hold enough issuer/program collateral and approve against an atomic internal credit reservation, then borrow and settle onchain after approval. Reap explicitly tells partners to reserve funds atomically and reconcile asynchronous events. This is a real route, although it gives up the stronger “finalized before approval” claim. Test both modes in the issuer sandbox with concurrent swipes, delayed RPCs, reversals, partial/over-clearing and refunds. [Reap handling](https://docs.reap.global/transactions/external-authorization/handling-requests.md), [Rain program models](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/launch-a-card-program-with-rain).

**What predecessors actually prove:** ether.fi documents automatic borrowing as card purchases occur and supplies `UserSafe`/debt-manager contracts. More decisively, Optimism's case study of ether.fi's **April 2026** migration says cards kept authorizing from an independent accounting ledger while assets were in transit and settled onchain later. That proves ether.fi can operate an **approve-then-settle** path, at least during migration; it does not establish whether every ordinary swipe uses that path. Gnosis describes an accessible-balance check and an onchain Roles transfer triggered **at the same time** as the Visa request, then an end-of-day issuer settlement; it does not prove that finality precedes approval. Exa's plugin gives a keeper `collectCredit()` and issuer validation; public docs do not specify the network-decision ordering. None is evidence that a finalized loan is required before every network approval. [ether.fi Borrow mode](https://help.ether.fi/en/articles/764912-card-spending-mode-borrow-mode-vs-direct-pay), [ether.fi contracts](https://github.com/etherfi-protocol/cash-contracts), [ether.fi migration case study](https://optimism.io/case-studies/etherfi), [Gnosis transaction guide](https://www.gnosis.io/blog/a-hackers-guide-to-gnosis-pay), [Exa plugin](https://docs.exact.ly/exa-app/exa-plugin).

**Wallet provisioning:** card acceptance in Apple Pay/Google Pay does not automatically give our app an “Add to Wallet” button. For iOS, obtain the card issuer/network tokenization setup and Apple's special `com.apple.developer.payment-pass-provisioning` entitlement, then use PassKit `PKAddPaymentPassViewController` with issuer-provided encrypted provisioning data. For Android, use Google's issuer Unified Push Provisioning API and token-service-provider integration; Google specifies server-side signing/encryption of the card object. Rain's public pages do not confirm that a new partner receives these APIs or the Apple entitlement automatically; ask Rain for BIN/geography eligibility, token-service-provider role, SDK/API, activation/ID&V, and the precise Apple and Google approval path. A manual add in Wallet is a distinct fallback flow. [Apple PassKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/passkit/pkaddpaymentpassviewcontroller), [Apple entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Miscellaneous/Reference/EntitlementKeyReference/ApplePayandPassKitEntitlements/ApplePayandPassKitEntitlements.html), [Google issuer APIs](https://developers.google.com/pay/issuers/apis), [Google payment-card provisioning](https://developers.google.com/pay/issuers/apis/push-provisioning/android/push-provisioning-google-opc).

**Card debt and liquidation:** value collateral with asset haircuts and independent fresh oracles; set an initial card-credit limit below the liquidation threshold; reserve approved amounts including FX/incidentals buffer; forbid withdrawing collateral backing card holds or margin; accrue borrowing cost; show health and permit voluntary repayment. If equity falls, cancel risk-increasing orders, reduce trading positions, then auction/sell collateral to repay stablecoin debt. The issuer must still be paid if the card has already been approved, so the funded credit/insurance pool absorbs any shortfall and has first-priority repayment rights. Ether.fi's published Borrow design confirms per-asset LTV, a health display and automated liquidation as a working pattern. The precise haircuts/thresholds are governance and stress-test decisions, **not sourced universal constants**. [ether.fi Borrow mechanics](https://help.ether.fi/en/articles/764754-how-borrow-works), [Reap reservation and clearing](https://docs.reap.global/transactions/external-authorization/handling-requests.md).

**Rain onboarding route:** form an entity, prepare beneficial-owner/source-of-funds records, scope target geographies, card type, permitted assets and partner-managed vs Rain-managed flows; request a Rain commercial call, then NDA, API access and sandbox; implement KYC/AML responsibilities agreed with Rain; prove the card lifecycle and authorization ledger; obtain compliance, card design and launch approval; pilot with controlled real users. Rain says partners *typically* launch in **around six weeks**, a provider claim, not a promise for this product. Rain's Agentic Startup Program is about agentic cards and should not be assumed to admit a consumer borrow-to-spend app. Initial ask: **Does Rain allow partner-managed auth backed by a Monad vault, what is our response deadline, and can its real-time-funding path compose with this lending flow?** [Rain onboarding guide](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/launch-a-card-program-with-rain), [Rain–Monad](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/rain-integrates-monad-bringing-card-programs-to-the-financial-layer-of-the-internet).

### B. A market layer needs executable liquidity

**Component contract:** (1) `MarketFactory/Registry` validates feed, session policy, leverage, open-interest cap and deployer stake; (2) bond escrow/slashing with objective violations, challenge evidence, appeal/governance policy and no discretionary seizure; (3) execution engine with order matching or a deliberately chosen RFQ/pool model; (4) oracle adapter validating feed signatures/proofs, freshness, confidence, timestamp, market session, gap limits and a fall-back reference; (5) mark/index and funding accumulators; (6) collateral vault and margin engine; (7) liquidator/auction; (8) insurance and bad-debt waterfall; (9) keeper/relayer monitoring; (10) indexer and mobile UI. HIP-3's documented requirement was **500,000 HYPE stake** when published; that is a *reference design*, not a sensible unexamined Monad bond size. Slashing requires evidence and governance, not just an admin button. [HIP-3](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals).

**Oracle lesson:** a fast block cannot invent a fresh stock print while its underlying market is closed. Pyth explicitly follows asset-class sessions and warns that stale or carried-forward equity prices must not be treated as executable. Its Pro payload distinguishes an update's publication time from the last time that feed actually produced a price. Use separate reference and executable marks: close/increase only with an eligible session and fresh feed; allow reduce-only with conservative bounds when appropriate; pause or limit liquidations through a gap until a valid reopening price; handle splits and symbol changes explicitly. A weekend synthetic mark based on an internal order book is *not* the official cash-equity price and needs tight OI/spread controls. The claimed **per-block funding advantage is weak**: accrue funding from time/position state, settle on position updates, and use fast finality for execution and liquidation instead. [Pyth best practices](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/best-practices), [market hours](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/pro/market-hours), [payload freshness](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/pro/payload-reference), [corporate actions](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/pro/understanding-price-data).

**Source-code route:** Synthetix V3's archived perps code is MIT and useful for margin/funding patterns, but needs active security maintenance. GMX Synthetics provides a well-documented pool model; its repository's `LICENSE` is BUSL-1.1 with a stated **31 August 2026** change date to GPL-2.0-or-later (or an earlier ENS date). Verify the exact version and effective grant before copying or choosing the project's output licence. Kuru's spot CLOB is not a perp engine and its public contracts should be read as a design reference unless reuse permission is obtained. Perpl's public SDK is an integration reference; public evidence does not grant a permissionless listing API. [Synthetix licence](https://github.com/Synthetixio/synthetix-v3/blob/main/LICENSE), [GMX licence](https://github.com/gmx-io/gmx-synthetics/blob/main/LICENSE), [GMX mechanics](https://github.com/gmx-io/gmx-synthetics), [HIP-3 documentation](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals).

**Liquidity route, not a passive hope:** choose a first market that professional makers can hedge in a deep external venue (gold or a liquid US equity/index); contract with makers for two-sided quoted depth and uptime; budget maker rebates or measured incentives; seed an insurance/backstop vault; cap OI by actual depth; then bring traders through a focused mobile app and broker/referral distribution. An own desk can quote first, but needs capital, hedging and independent limits. Hyperliquid's documented route combines permissionless market making, volume-based maker rebates and a community-funded HLP vault that both quotes and liquidates; HIP-3 deployers inherited that infrastructure. Alternatively choose GMX's pool-as-counterparty route, where LPs earn fees and absorb trader P&L, but that changes the product from CLOB to vault-based execution. A mobile trading app on existing liquidity is a valid **distribution-first entry point**, but by itself it would not deliver the permissionless market layer. Build the app around one market and connect it to our own venue; use existing-venue routing for other assets only if transparently labelled. [Hyperliquid market making](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/market-making), [maker fees/rebates](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees), [HLP](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/protocol-vaults), [HIP-3](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals), [trade[XYZ] architecture](https://docs.trade.xyz/), [GMX LP model](https://docs.gmx.io/docs/providing-liquidity/).

**Regulatory operating route:** perps on stock/FX references and a Visa card are different regulated relationships. Pick an initial permitted jurisdiction with specialist counsel, identify who runs the derivatives venue/market access and who issues the card, restrict disallowed locations and sanctions, document that cash-settled perps confer no share ownership, and obtain market-data commercial rights. Existing offshore venues and issuer partners show possible structures, but there is **no evidence that their permissions transfer to this team**. Partner and jurisdiction approvals are concrete gates to clear. [Perpl public market context](https://app.perpl.xyz/api/v1/pub/context), [Rain onboarding/compliance](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/launch-a-card-program-with-rain), [trade[XYZ] architecture](https://docs.trade.xyz/).

### C. The one-balance design and the actual user journey

**User:** a mobile-native investor who holds liquid crypto/stablecoins, trades a small set of gold and equity perps, and wants everyday spending without manually moving money between apps. “Everyone who wants a card and stocks” is too broad for the Track 01 readiness criterion. The first cohort should be named by jurisdiction and demonstrated behavior during user interviews.

**One vault, one risk engine:** for each account, calculate `adjusted equity = haircut-adjusted collateral + conservative realized/unrealized P&L − card debt − fees`; calculate `available = adjusted equity − required trading margin − unsettled card holds − safety buffer`. Each new order, withdrawal or card authorization must update a common state atomically or be serialized against the same onchain nonce/lock. “One collateral balance” means a single *risk-accounted* pool, **not that the same dollar is spendable twice**. At card authorization, reserve the obligation first; if approved, release stablecoins from a funded credit pool/escrow or create card debt under an enforceable repayment right. A merchant tip, delayed capture or refund changes the hold on reconciliation. In falling markets, cancel orders and shrink positions before the vault becomes insolvent; freeze new card credit while oracle state is stale. These are design requirements derived from the issuer's hold rules and trading-margin principles, not claims that a deployed protocol already has them. [Reap handling](https://docs.reap.global/transactions/external-authorization/handling-requests.md), [Bybit unified-margin example](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/FAQ-Unified-Trading-Account), [ether.fi liquidation example](https://help.ether.fi/en/articles/764754-how-borrow-works).

**Happy path:** passkey sign-in → deposit AUSD or eligible collateral → see one portfolio with **free to trade / free to spend / encumbered** amounts → open a gold or equity perp with a live, session-labelled quote → final fill and health update → tap card → card hold immediately reduces free collateral, while the trading position stays open → clear/repay and release any unused hold. **Failure paths:** stale/closed equity feed blocks new risk; insufficient *free* equity declines card without touching existing margin; RPC/issuer timeout follows the program's defined fail-closed or prefunded fallback; merchant reversal releases the hold; price gap triggers reduce-only and staged liquidation; issuer outage leaves the onchain portfolio intact but card unavailable. These flows should be tested before broadening markets.

## Recommended implementation contract and sequence

**Client and services.** React Native/Expo with native development builds; Mera WebAuthn PRF through its React Native adapter for passkey sign-in and an EVM signing session; biometric device unlock for short sessions; `viem` for simulation and explicit gas limits; a backend for KYC handoff, card webhook, idempotent ledger, issuer secrets and push events; Envio/another Monad event indexer for fills, holds, funding and liquidations. Use `finalized` for economic confirmation; do not call a speculative `latest` receipt a settled trade. Mera's preview API and device/passkey support must be verified on the actual target devices. [Mera repository](https://github.com/category-labs/mera), [Monad real-time data](https://docs.monad.xyz/monad-arch/realtime-data), [Monad indexers](https://docs.monad.xyz/guides/indexers), [Monad differences](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/differences.md).

**Contracts.** `AccountVault` (collateral and withdrawals); `CollateralOracle` (haircuts and freshness); `RiskEngine` (one equity/obligation state); `MarketFactory` and deployer stake/slashing; `SessionOracle`/mark and corporate-action policy; `PerpEngine` (matching or selected execution model, positions, funding); `LiquidationEngine`; `InsuranceFund`; `CardHoldDebt` (issuer-authorized holds, draw, repayment, reversal, expiry); `CreditPool` (stablecoin liquidity owed to issuer); scoped operator/session permissions and emergency pause. Own the market engine if permissionless listing is a core claim; Perpl integration can be a separate route only after it agrees to list or expose the needed interface. [HIP-3](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals), [Exa permission model](https://docs.exact.ly/exa-app/exa-plugin), [Reap reconciliation](https://docs.reap.global/transactions/external-authorization/reconciliation.md).

**Order:**

1. Write the named user journey, jurisdiction/partner map, solvency equations and loss waterfall. Obtain a commercial oracle feed/rights plan and issuer requirements; define market sessions and treatment of gaps, splits, holidays and stale data. This is product discovery and protocol design, not a delivery-time filter.
2. Implement and property-test the vault/risk invariant: no combination of new order, withdrawal, card hold, delayed capture, liquidation and refund can create double-spendable collateral. Model concurrent calls and oracle failure.
3. Ship one fully settled, session-aware market with deposits, orders, fills, funding, liquidation and insurance accounting on Monad, then a mobile trade journey with passkey sign-in and clear free-equity displays.
4. Establish actual liquidity: quote agreement, two-sided depth, hedge route, maker incentives, OI caps and monitoring. Measure executable spread and uptime, not only simulated trade count.
5. Integrate the issuer sandbox. Implement signature verification, idempotent holds, all transaction lifecycle events and a latency harness. Attempt final-before-approve borrowing against the real contracted deadline; use prefunded credit reservation if it does not meet that threshold reliably. Complete KYB/KYC, compliance and card-program approval with the issuer.
6. Implement Apple/Google issuer provisioning after token-service-provider and entitlement approvals; conduct physical-device, real-card and clearing/reversal tests. Only then describe the product as spendable at merchants.

**Acceptance evidence for the recommendation:** verified Monad trade and risk transitions; source code and tests for double-spend invariants; oracle session/gap tests; a quoted market with external maker or funded pool; mobile iOS/Android passkey recovery; an issuer sandbox authorization with p50/p99 timing and reversal/over-clear reconciliation; then separately, a live issuer-approved card and physical merchant transactions. A sandbox webhook proves integration, not card issuance; a fast block proves chain performance, not whole authorization latency.
