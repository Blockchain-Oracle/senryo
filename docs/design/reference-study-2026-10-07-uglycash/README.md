## Important findings

- Native canvas and sheet fill repeatedly sample **#F5F5F5**, white cards **#FFFFFF**, vivid accent **#FA00FF**, input fills around **#ECECEC**. These are dominant JPEG pixel observations, not recovered design tokens. Refero's marketing style instead says #F2F2F2; native pixels win for the app.
- U14-S17 shows an app-owned Face ID primer over a dimmed Home; S18 is the genuine iOS permission alert; S19 returns to Home. U14-S11–12 separately show Sign in with Apple and its biometric ceremony. These are distinct authentication steps.
- U01 is named “Adding Bitcoin to portfolio” but shows USDT, amount entry, a slide, processing, and a **sell** success scene. It proves neither BTC purchase nor supported BTC receiving rails.
- U02 stops at Apple Pay processing; it does not show crypto delivery. U11 stops at a recipient-search failure; it does not show a completed send. U06 shows acknowledgement, export buttons and copy feedback, but no readable secret or biometric gate.
- The source has materially different environments: light trading surfaces, blue-sky onboarding/profile/success art, and a textured monochrome Send workspace. Flattening them into one gray page would lose fidelity.
- Profile-link sharing, a rendered club invitation and a rendered trade card are three distinct artifacts. Their captions, privacy and routing cannot be collapsed into one generic Share button.
- Small white text on sampled magenta has approximately 3.23:1 contrast; black on magenta is 6.51:1. Readable control text is an explicit accessibility adaptation, not a reason to mute the accent.
