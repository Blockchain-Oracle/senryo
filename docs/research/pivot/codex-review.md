# Codex (gpt-6.1-sol, xhigh) adversarial review of the draft pivot plan — 8 Oct 2026

Input: the draft plan before the review fixes. Every P0 item is folded into D-261…D-267.

**Do not approve the fixed-odds design for mainnet yet.** Freshness, spread and caps do not establish safe execution or solvency. Make these changes in priority order. Numerical defaults below are proposed testnet starting points.

1. **P0 — Resolve the funding contradiction before implementation.** With no substantial house funding, recommend **Up/Down parimutuel for mainnet**: variable final odds, fixed betting cutoff, refunds for one-sided pools, no guaranteed cash-out. Keep the house model on testnet. At 3% odds, before spread, a $25 stake creates roughly $833 gross payout liability. Public LP deposits transfer that risk to users; they do not remove it. Instant fills, fixed odds and unconditional exits require funded capital.

2. **P0 — Replace the claim that “3 seconds prevents sniping.”** Attackers can observe exchange moves, select an older-but-valid Pyth print, buy cheaply, then close against a newer print. Monotonic timestamps prevent rollback but cannot prove the newest global update was supplied. Prefer **on-chain commitment followed by deterministic execution**: escrow funds; target `commitTime + 1s`; use the unique first update in `[target,target+5s]`; allow permissionless finalization; prohibit discretionary cancellation after commitment. Preserve future-publication rejection and the 3-second ceiling for immediate pricing paths. Require ≥3 seconds holding and a newer publication before cash-out; stop **both opens and cash-outs at expiry−20s** initially. Disclose that cutoff. Spread and holding periods alone cannot eliminate directional latency arbitrage. [Pyth’s threat model](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/best-practices).

3. **P0 — Specify one pricing model and remove executable clamps.** Under the stated zero-log-drift model, band probability is  
   `Φ(ln(H/S)/v) − Φ(ln(L/S)/v)`, with `v=σ√τ` and explicit infinite endpoints. [RangeMath](/Users/abu/dev/hackathon/sommina-events/contracts/src/range/RangeMath.sol:73) instead uses arithmetic returns and a book-derived centre; [fair.ts](/Users/abu/dev/hackathon/owarine/packages/core/src/market/fair.ts:45) defaults to an equity variance clock and ties-Up. Neither is a drop-in port. Specify volatility units, crypto/calendar versus equity/session clocks, estimator, calibration bounds and immutable configuration versions. Sigma errors can misprice either direction; finite Range probabilities have interior extrema. Price asks against the maximum probability across calibrated spot/sigma uncertainty, bids against the minimum, with an initial **2-percentage-point half-spread** plus bounded inventory surcharge. **Reject** new positions outside raw 3–97%; never floor losing bids at 3% or cap near-certain asks at 97%. Those clamps manufacture arbitrage.

4. **P0 — Write the actual liability invariant.** For remaining ticket `i`, define payout `qᵢ`, remaining original stake basis `bᵢ`, and terminal obligation `fᵢ(x)` including exact-boundary refunds:
   
   `Lw = max(max_x Σᵢ fᵢ(x), Σᵢ bᵢ [void])`  
   `houseReserve_w = max(0, Lw − remainingStakeEscrow_w)`.
   
   Up, Range and Moonshot can win together: neither `max(Up,Down)` nor the largest individual band is sufficient. Across windows require **actual USDC balance ≥ unpaid credits + Σ unresolved Lw**, without double-counting credits. Four fixed bands have at most four distinct thresholds: maintain aggregates over **five intervals and four singleton endpoints**, plus void; updates are bounded independently of ticket count. Prohibit arbitrary user endpoints initially. Alternatively reserve each ticket’s full payout conservatively. Recheck solvency **after closes**, because paying cash while removing an offsetting position can destroy collateral coverage. Unconditional exits require sufficient additional reserve. Queue LP entry/exit pricing through settled epochs; `liquid+locked` at par conceals known losses before claims.

5. **P0 — Define ties, endpoints and round-trip invariants.** Recommend refunding **only Up/Down** at exactly K; Range wins on its declared inclusive endpoints, Moonshot excludes its strike. Oracle prices are discrete: tie probability is not automatically zero. With original-stake tie refunds, fair entry probability becomes `pWin/(1−pTie)`. Preserve original basis through partial closes. Same-state close/reopen should lose spread; exploitation arises from changing sigma, asymmetric clamps, rounding, recycled refund basis or rebates. Require identical configuration, `bid≤ask`, floor payouts, ceil debits, and no volume rewards for recycled stakes.

6. **P0 — Repair session authorization before enabling prompt-free calls.** [Typed-data policy](/Users/abu/dev/hackathon/metropolis/packages/account/src/policy/typed-data.ts:27) currently checks domain/type/user only; [signTypedData](/Users/abu/dev/hackathon/metropolis/packages/account/src/session/signer.ts:83) neither records spend nor checks usage. Serialize **evaluation → budget reservation → signing**, including pending signatures. Enforce exact field schemas and amounts. Suggested real-mode limits: $25/call, $100 aggregate/session, 15-minute expiry. Current Mera sessions expose the owner’s signing authority; client-side restrictions do not cryptographically constrain a compromised key. Use an on-chain restricted trading delegate if claiming enforceable session scope.

7. **P0 — Complete intent and nonce semantics.** Sign action, owner, token, window, exact band, amount/quantity, maximum debit, minimum shares/output, recipient, configuration version, deadline, fee ceiling and authorization epoch; domain-bind chain and verifying contract. Use unordered owner-scoped nonces shared by direct/relayed execution, explicit cancellation and epoch invalidation. Consume before external interactions under reentrancy protection. Bound ERC-1271 validation. Simulate the **exact calldata and transaction value**, including oracle fee/quality, allowance/balance, ownership, remaining quantity, cutoff, slippage, capacity and pending sponsor transactions. Re-simulate before submission. Signed limits and deterministic execution constrain sandwiches; relayers can still censor, reorder or exhaust capacity. [EIP-712 does not supply replay/front-running protection](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-712).

8. **P0 — Separate permit, wallet transfers and vault withdrawals.** A permit deadline expires the signature, **not the resulting allowance**. Use finite allowances, explicit revocation, and tolerate permit front-running when sufficient allowance already exists. Test native USDC’s actual domain and signature overloads. EIP-3009 can transfer **wallet-held USDC**; a user cannot authorize spending USDC owned by HouseVault. Vault credits need a signed withdrawal that debits credit before transferring. Bind recipient, amount, validity bounds and token nonce. For wrapped deposits use `receiveWithAuthorization`; extracted `transferWithAuthorization` signatures can bypass the wrapper and strand accounting. [ERC-3009](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-3009).

9. **P1 — Make lazy opening deterministic and keep a keeper.** First call must attach the unique **start-boundary proof**, never establish K from its current spot. Preserve [the existing unique parser](/Users/abu/dev/hackathon/metropolis/contracts/src/predictions/PythBoundaryOracle.sol:40) for opening and settlement. Archive proofs independently of the primary relayer. Set complementary admission/void deadlines, immutable quality rejection, and idempotent terminal states. Fresh equity prices do not guarantee a fresh future settlement: registry admission must prevent windows crossing holidays, early closes or session end. Define halt/corporate-action handling.
